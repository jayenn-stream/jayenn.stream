Streams of thought that have occurred at some point in the ongoing life of someone named Jayenn, and that are another mind's attempt to keep up with reality.

In expressing streams of thought, I do not burden myself with the need of logical continuity from sentence to sentence (or phrase to phrase), because that is not how thought generally goes on.

To this end, I adopt some novel syntactical forms. The main one is this: anywhere I decide not to decide on a new thought's relation to the prior one, I use a five space to separate rather than a single one:

Like this.     Maybe, there are other reasons. There might be other reasons why I use the five space.     Maybe it's when I don't want to commit.     Maybe that's the same thing.     Oh, maybe it's when I want to permit myself not to worry about it, so that I can maintain flow, instead of getting into the stop and start, mostly stop, of deliberation.     5-Spaces may be symbolic too perhaps. Symbolic not to worry about all the details that you normally see that stultifies you. Including being repetitive, as I am now.     The word stultify is too posh. I wish there was a more plain one.     I believe Orwell advocated using plain over sophisticated language.     Being too plain is a vice too. Or, I should say, being too...     [I can't think of a sophisticated word for plain, damn]     ...is a vice too.

So, reader, you may expect that wherever there is a five space separating a sentence or phrase, the next sentence may have nothing at all apparent to do with the preceding one. Its relation was merely that I thought it. Or, it may have an apparent relation. Either way, knowing that the clarity of logical continuity in prose is not pretended, you should be less confused (I hope).

A second convention I use is a five dot, to indicate a sidetracked thought entering, and returning back to the main thought. Suppose I'm thinking something about A. When something tangential comes up I segue out of the main thought with five ellipses.....     Then do my incidental thought.     .....then return to the main line.
